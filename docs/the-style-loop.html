The Style Loop

One controller, a trained network it calls as a tool, three sheets fitted from the person's own archive, and a locked testbed that says whether what comes out reads as him.

alive / engines / knowledge_style · 18 September 2026 · design document

This describes the style half of the system: the part that takes the substance of an answer and renders it as him speaking. It begins where The Content Loop stops and ends at the voice. It follows that document section for section, so the two can be read against each other. The evaluators and the testbed are built and measured. The harness is proposed. Every claim says which it is.

The whole shape, before any part of it

It emits two renders of one string. The voice hears the stumbles; the subtitle does not. That split is the reason disfluency can exist at all as text: without it, the thing that makes the audio sound like him makes the caption look broken.

The invariant: one intelligence, two ways in

The content loop's invariant was two intelligences and two creator routes. Here there is one intelligence, and the change is deliberate.

The style network is not a second intelligence. It is a trained map from text to text with no prompt at all — its own code calls this the no-prompt-at-inference contract, and the input is wrapped in a chat template with no system message. It cannot be reasoned with, only parameterised. So exactly one thing in this loop forms an opinion: the controller.

The chain, and what is adjustable in it

The markup, and why there are two renders

Disfluency exists so the audio sounds like him. The same string is also the subtitle. Those two want different text, and the speech-markup standard already has the element that resolves it.

source     it's <sub alias="a a core human desire">a core human desire</sub>.<break time="400ms"/>
spoken     it's a a core human desire. [400ms pause]
written    it's a core human desire.

The alias is what is spoken; the element's text content is what survives into the written form. bookmark is not spoken and raises an event carrying its offset in the audio stream, which is subtitle timing. break takes a strength (x-weak 250ms through x-strong 1250ms) or an exact time up to 20,000ms, and mstts:silence places exact silence at nine named positions.

a correction to this repo's own note

references/SURVEY.md §16 states that markup cannot carry a stammer because no element repeats a word. The first half is right and the conclusion is wrong: no element generates a repeat, but sub carries one, because the spoken form and the written form are different attributes of the same element. problems.md N16 leans on the wrong version. Both need amending.

Two things still have to be checked rather than assumed: this is Azure's dialect, while the voice provider currently in use is ElevenLabs, whose newer model replaced break tags with bracketed cues; and the page states that personal voices do not support all SSML tags, which is exactly the category a cloned voice falls into.

Two models, and the wall between them

The content loop's wall is between the controller and its judge: one reasons, one reports, and neither sees the other's work. Here the wall runs somewhere else, and it matters more.

The wall is that the network knows nothing about the task. It has never seen a question, a passage or an instruction. That is what makes it swappable and what makes it dumb: it cannot be told to preserve a number, so the thing that catches it reversing one has to sit outside it.

Two of these are load-bearing. The controller cannot see its score, which is what keeps the number an evaluation instead of a training signal. And the content check knows nothing about style, which is why it can be trusted as the guard: nothing a style improvement does can flatter it.

Four findings, and which step each one implicates

All four are measured on the locked testbed. Together they say the design above is roughly right and that one of its three stages is much smaller than it looks.

One. What gives the machine away is structure, not vocabulary

The style judge must name a category and quote the evidence. Over 87 machine answers:

Nothing is cited about rhythm and almost nothing about vocabulary. 71% of it is how the argument is shaped. That is mostly a prompt problem, which is the cheapest kind, and it reorders the priorities the three-stage plan started with.

Two. The biggest tell is only half mechanical

A rule for labelled openers, ordinal scaffolds and inline signposts fires on 38 of 87 machine answers against 8 of 87 of his. Real signal, worth gating. But against what the judge means by SCAFFOLDED it is weak: it catches 19 of the 36 it labelled that way, and fires on 19 of the 51 it labelled otherwise.

So the gate removes a visible difference and does not cover the finding. The rest is in how the argument is built, which only step 1 can change.

Three. A stammer is worth about a fifth of the distance

They are measured off transcripts, and the transcriber dropped disfluency, bleeped words and mis-transcribed others before we saw the text. The layer that would carry his real speech with timing intact does not exist in this system. 0.40 disfluencies per 100 words across the archive is what was written down, not what he did — and a cloned voice learns from the audio, not from this.

The three controls

The content loop has twelve. This has three, and the difference is not that this system is simpler: it is that most of what looks like a control here is a gate, and a gate is not something the controller sets.

#ControlThe controller choosesAttacks
1how to use the incoming passageswhich framing, how many of his passages to read, whether to try another framing after a rejectionSCAFFOLDED 41%, BALANCED 20%, SUMMARISES 10%, REGISTER 6%
2how to drive the networkwhich adapter, decoding, how the input is segmented, whether to send it againREGISTER, looping, ARRIVES_FINISHED
3disfluency placementwhether this answer stumbles, and whereARRIVES_FINISHED 14%

Control 2 has no prompt in it, which is the thing most likely to be got wrong. The adapter takes no system message, so "prompt the network differently" is not available; what is available is temperature, top-p, repetition penalty, the no-repeat window, which checkpoint, and how a long input is split before rewriting.

That last one is worth a test rather than an assumption. Inputs over a word limit are sentence-split, rewritten independently, and joined. Segments rewritten in isolation and stitched is a plausible source of the self-contained, tidily-closing paragraphs the judge flags at 41% and 20%. Varying the split alone would say.

Two of these are bands rather than caps, and that shape is what makes them ungameable. The content loop caps answer length because shorter is never a fault; here an answer at 60 words is as unlike him as one at 400. Same for the habit band: overshooting his filler rate fails it exactly as undershooting does, which is why the earlier authorship metric could be won by repeating one word fifty times and these cannot.

The wall clock is off deliberately, the same call the content loop made: a budget from the start mixes "how good can this get" with "how good can it get in a minute". Quality first, then read the latency it actually needs and set the budget from that.

the knob that is already turned past its end

Judged by distance to his stylistic centre, in units of his own spread, the adapter sits at 3.16 and he sits at 3.25. The machine is closer to his average than he is, and it is still identified as a machine essentially every time.

He is a spread, not a point. Minimising distance to his centre produces something more average-him than him, which is the flattening the whole problem is made of. Judged instead by distance between the two distributions, the same adapter is worse than doing nothing: 154 against 128, with his own floor at 23. Two readings of the same twelve numbers, opposite answers — which is why "maximise the style metric" has no referent yet.

Requirement one: the controller must understand its own machinery

The content controller is handed a manual of its own stages, because a model that does not know what a control does cannot reach for it. The same holds here, and there is one fact it must have that is counter-intuitive enough to state twice.

One stage, as the controller actually receives it

## restyle

Hands a passage to a model trained on this person's speech. Text in, text out.

IT TAKES NO INSTRUCTION. There is no prompt, no system message, nothing you
can ask it for. Wording your request differently changes nothing, because your
words are not sent. What you can change:

  which checkpoint   several exist and they fail differently
  decoding           greedy loops a phrase in roughly a quarter of outputs.
                     Escalating ONLY the outputs that loop kept content at
                     0.927; a blanket setting dropped it to 0.859
  segmentation       a long input is sentence-split, each part rewritten
                     ALONE, and the parts joined. Suspect this when the result
                     reads as a list of self-contained paragraphs
  send it again      its output can be fed back through

IT WILL SOMETIMES REVERSE A CLAIM. Measured at one in five outputs on an
earlier checkpoint, while every automatic score read fine. Read what comes
back against what you sent. This is why you run after it and not only before.

The manual carries the mechanism and none of his facts: the archive's size, his rates and his vocabulary are filled in from whichever creator is loaded, and the file is linted for creator-specific content exactly as the content side's twelve prompt files are.

Requirement two: the controller must turn findings into an ordered plan

The style judge returns three fields per failure, and the third is the one that makes a plan possible.

tell     SCAFFOLDED
evidence "Short answer: no. On the living situation: ... The other thing"
instead  "And so this this sense of compassion also comes in, right? Like
          you start to realize that, oh, maybe this person is..."

The second and third fields are a pair. What the machine did, and what he does at that moment, quoted from his own archive. That is the whole reason the judge is shown 86,000 words: not to help it decide, but to say what should have happened instead.

The ordering rule carries over from the content loop unchanged, because the reason for it is unchanged: group the findings by what would fix them, and re-reach before re-writing. Several tells share one cause — scaffolding, balanced clauses and a summarising close are frequently one decision about how to open — and a controller that fixes them one at a time spends its budget discovering that.

Two bounds, both measured on the content side: one retry, not two (across six retried questions the second was wasted four times, and the two apparent saves were recovering from the first retry making things worse), and the judge is not in the loop by default, because a judging step inside it measured 20 seconds of a 60-second budget.

Sheet three: the prompt library

The framings control 1 chooses between. The only sheet fitted on answered questions, and therefore the only one that forces a development split. It is also the sheet that should be written first and fitted later, because the judge already says what is wrong: scaffolding 41%, balanced clauses 20%, summarising closes 10%.

What fitting actually does, in order

  1. Measure the judge's rerun noise first. Identical inputs, identical settings, twice. On the content side this was skipped and it cost five prompt variants that were unresolvable by construction: rerunning the same configuration moved 17 of 20 question scores and flipped 7 pass/fail. The across-question noise here is known and healthy — a 1.1-point move is readable out of 75 points of headroom — but that is a different number and does not substitute.
  2. Fit the two archive sheets. No questions touched, so nothing is spent and no split is contaminated.
  3. Write the first prompts against the diagnosis, do not search for them. The judge already says scaffolding 41%, balanced 20%, summarising 10%. With 87 questions, writing against a named failure is far more sample-efficient than fitting, and it has a target where a search would be groping.
  4. Measure each arm alone before any controller wraps them: the network by itself, prompting by itself, both in series. Three arms, no training. A controller wrapping two unmeasured stages cannot be debugged.
  5. Only then fit the prompt library, if the written prompts have stopped improving.

What is measured, in plain terms

Two evaluators, and neither is allowed to stand alone. One asks whether it reads as him. The other asks whether it still says what it was handed. An arm can win either while losing the other badly.

EvaluatorThe question it answersReported as
the style judgeshown his archive and six passages, three his and three the arm's, from six different questions: which are him?the arm's mean rating, with the floor beside it
the content checkthe four measures the content loop already ships, re-run on the styled textthe delta against the same four on the unstyled answer

The content check writes no measure of its own. It produces a report in the shape the existing scorer consumes, carrying the styled text and the original run's retrieval untouched — the style stage does not retrieve, so scoring it against a freshly retrieved set would credit or blame it for retrieval it never did. That also gives a free self-check: context recall must not move at all, and if it does, the bridge is broken rather than the arm.

Which readout to steer on, which to report

The style judge was measured three ways on the same locked testbed and the same arm. The differences decide which number goes where.

  • The pile saturated it, not the archive. Going from one-at-a-time to a lineup moved AUC 0.974 to 0.999 by itself; the whole archive on top moved it 0.999 to 1.000.
  • What the archive bought is confidence and reasons. The arm's rating fell 14.8 to 10.2, and only with the archive can the judge quote what he does instead.
  • Requiring reasons made it less certain, and that is an improvement. Floor 0.0% back up to 3.4%. Having to name a category and quote evidence stops it committing freely.
  • AUC is not the readout. Pinned at 0.999 it cannot report progress. The arm's mean rating can: 9.2 today, 84.7 to climb to, and a 1.1-point move is readable.
  • A judge sorting on word count alone scores 0.864 on this same split. The judge beats a ruler by 0.135, so it is reading more than length — and that margin is the thing to watch, not the headline.

Rules that keep the measurement honest

  1. The testbed is locked by a hash of its three frozen columns — the question, the input, the reference. Adding an arm as a new field is legal; changing a question, an input or a reference is refused, loudly, before anything is scored. Verified by tampering with one row and confirming the judge refuses.
  2. Typography is scrubbed in code, identically on both sides. Before it, the em dash appeared in 95 of 100 machine answers and 3 of his. An instruction only covers a cue a judge would have named; a typographic tell can be used silently and then explained as something else.
  3. Length is not normalised, deliberately. Truncating one side to match the other would change the rhythm being measured. It is forbidden in the prompt and verified in the verdicts: 0 of 174 named a forbidden cue, and the correlation of word count with rating is −0.12 on the machine side.
  4. The judge never sees the marked-up string, only the spoken render. Angle brackets would be a giant tell.
  5. Nothing is fitted on the set that is reported. Three sheets get fitted on questions, so the 87 become a development set the moment fitting starts, and a second disjoint draw is locked for reporting.
  6. Distinct answers of his, not distinct questions. The first build had 100 questions carrying only 87 different answers of his, because several questions are answered by the same stretch of a take.
  7. Read the verdicts. Every instrument on this axis that was not read turned out to be measuring something else.
the instrument that nearly lied, and how it was caught

The counter that verifies the judge ignored transcript artifacts first reported 36% of verdicts in violation. It was matching "mid-sentence false start" and "self-correction" — which are the style tells, not the banned ones. Reading all 195 verdicts found none, and the corrected rate is 0 of 174.

Separately, the first run silently dropped five verdicts, all from one side, because a strict JSON parse failed on quotes inside free prose. A one-sided loss is the shape of bias no summary statistic shows.

Why the evaluators cannot be the same judge

The content loop proves its harness equivalent to the deployed path in two lines. The equivalent claim here is a separation, not an equivalence, and it is the one thing in this design that could silently fail.

The style judge is read by the controller. The reported number must not be. But both are the same family of model asked nearly the same question, and both key on the same cues. A controller tuned until the judge stops complaining is a controller tuned against the score, one model removed, and nothing in the numbers would show it.

Two things close it, and neither is optional once fitting starts:

  • A second locked testbed, a disjoint draw of questions, never fitted on. The 87 become development.
  • Measure how far the two agree on the same questions. If the judge's reasons predict the reported rating almost perfectly, they are one instrument under two names. If they disagree usefully, the critic is carrying information the score does not, which is the case for having it.

The control flow of one answer

# proposed. mirrors loop.py's shape, including what it deliberately omits

answer, passages = from the content run
msgs = [handed the answer, the passages, the style sheet]

while turns < HARD_TURNS:
    text, calls = controller(msgs)

    # the styled answer is PROSE, recognised wherever it appears.
    # requiring a tool call for it threw away 16 finished answers
    # on the content side, and each rewrite shed specifics.
    if "<styled>" in text:
        draft = parse(text)
        faults = gate_check(draft)        # ALL gates, one message
        if faults: msgs += [text, faults]; continue
        return draft                        # no judge in the path

    for c in calls:
        if c is hear:     out = his passages, by index
        if c is measure:  out = the twelve counters vs his bands
        if c is restyle: out = network(c.text, decoding=c.decoding)
        if c is break:   out = wrap the named spans, return both renders

    msgs += [blocks unchanged, results]     # thinking blocks replayed as-is

The network is a tool, the judges are not

On the content side the judge is forced rather than offered: a model given a judge as an optional tool will not call it when it is confident, and confidence is uncorrelated with being right. The same logic applies here with the opposite conclusion for each piece.

Where the code does not match the design

  • The one-at-a-time judge was frozen in METRICS.md as "the acceptance test" before the lineup existed. The document above now assigns the lineup the reporting role and keeps the one-at-a-time judge for the floor and the AUC. `METRICS.md` has not been amended to say so, and it is the owner.
  • lineup.py imports its scoring and its banned-cue list from detect.py, so the two cannot diverge — but that makes detect.py the owner of shared machinery while also being an instrument, which is the kind of double role that drifts.
  • SURVEY.md §16 and problems.md N16 both state that markup cannot carry a stammer. It can, via sub. Not yet corrected.
  • The testbed has no train/test split, which was right when nothing optimised against it.

The harness as it will be built

Guarantees are code. Choices are prompt.

Every gate in the table earlier exists because the prompt version of it is expected to lose, and on the content side that expectation was measured: asking the model not to invent a connecting sentence was tried twice and lost twice, while a code gate took unnumbered claims from 283 to zero. A guaranteed change also needs no statistics to verify, which matters most when the noise floor is wide.

The split here: quantity is code, placement is prompt. How many stumbles is his measured rate and the controller may not exceed it. Where they go is judgement and only the controller can decide it.

What gets deleted, and why

  • The five-verdict content judge I wrote for this stage. Deleted. It was a second content instrument with no justification, and it would have made a style regression and a content regression report in different units.
  • The pairwise style judge as a scoreboard item. Kept as machinery, never reported: 98–100% across four adapters, two base sizes, two datasets and five decoding settings.
  • Corpus-level style aggregates. A naive copy baseline wins them. Any aggregate is computed per passage and then averaged.
  • "Share of the gap closed" per feature. Monotone, therefore gameable, and the existing numbers include a 502% where an arm overshot.

The sanity check, before any quality claim

The content harness's rule: read what the model actually did on a handful of questions before believing any number from a hundred. Five behavioural questions, each with a specific thing to look for.

Is it following the flow, or doing its own thing?

Did it read any of his passages before writing, or go straight to the network? Did it call the network at all, or quietly answer in its own voice and skip the stage? The trace has to show the calls, not just the output.

Is it using the controls, and reacting to the feedback?

measure is free, so a controller that never calls it is leaving the only signal it has on the table. And when a gate hands something back, does the next draft change the thing that was named, or reshuffle wording until something passes?

Does it know the network takes no prompt?

The specific failure to look for: the controller writing an instruction into the text it sends to the network, as though the network would read it. That instruction is not just ignored, it is rewritten and returned as content, which is how a directive ends up inside a finished answer.

Did it act on the judge, or just retry?

The judge's reasons go back, not its labels, and the test is whether the next attempt addresses the named category or simply tries again. Grouping matters: if scaffolding, balanced clauses and a summarising close all came back, a controller that treats them as three problems is spending its budget on one.

Two behavioural faults to expect

  • Lifting his phrasing wholesale. The passages in front of it are his real words on this subject, so copying them scores beautifully on the style judge while importing claims the answer never made. Faithfulness and padding are the two content measures that would move, which is precisely why the content check is not optional.
  • Buying style with content. Compressing toward his length is a requirement, and the cheapest way to hit it is to drop a claim. The content check catches it; a style number alone never would.

Auditing the judge

Where it was right

  • It ignored everything it was told to ignore. 0 of 174 verdicts named length, censor marks, punctuation, truncation or topic, across three formats.
  • It beat a ruler. Word count alone scores 0.864 on the same split; the judge scores 0.999.
  • Its floor is coherent. The passages of his it calls machine are short ones where he genuinely speaks in finished sentences, and its stated reason is nearly identical each time. That is a real property of him, not judge error.
  • Its "instead" quotes are real. When it says what he does at that moment, it is quoting the archive in front of it rather than composing something plausible.

Where it was wrong, or where reading it changed the design

  • It over-committed until it was made to explain itself. Floor 0.0% and AUC 1.000 with reasons not required; 3.4% and 0.999 with them.
  • Length is still 86% of the way to its answer. Not a fault in the judge, but the margin over a ruler is the thing to watch as arms improve, not the headline.
  • The tell distribution contradicted the plan. The single-item judge's free-text reasons made disfluency look like the dominant problem. Forced to name a category, the same judge puts scaffolding at 41% and disfluency at 14%.

What the sanity check changed

Nothing yet — the harness does not exist. What reading the judge changed, before any harness was written:

  • The reported readout moved from AUC to the arm's mean rating, because AUC is pinned at 0.999 and cannot show progress while the rating has 75 points of headroom and 1.1-point resolution.
  • The content judge was deleted and replaced by a bridge to the shipped one.
  • Step ordering flipped: the controller runs after the network as well as before, because the network's failures need repair and only the controller can do it.
  • The priority list was reordered from disfluency-first to structure-first, on the tell distribution.
  • Markup moved from ruled-out to load-bearing, once the reason for disfluency was stated as audio rather than text.

What is not known

  • The judge's rerun noise. The single most important missing number, and one run away.
  • Whether prompting and the network stack or compete. Never tested together. The one measurement touching it says prompting alone is harmful.
  • Which adapter. Several on disk, all trained on the wrong input shape, none run at this seam.
  • Whether independent segment rewriting causes the scaffolding. Cheap to test, untested.
  • How much the judge's reasons and the reported rating agree. Decides whether the split is a formality or the only thing keeping the number honest.
  • Whether a cloned voice supports sub and bookmark, and which provider dialect we are committing to.
  • Whether a cloned voice produces hesitation by itself, in which case no text stage owns disfluency and step 3 should not exist.
  • What his real disfluency rate is. Every number here is measured off a transcriber's reading of the audio, and the layer that would carry the truth does not exist in this system.
