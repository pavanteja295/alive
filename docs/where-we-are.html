The thing being measured

Someone asks a question. The system finds passages from his transcripts and writes an answer. That is two model calls and one search.

1  What actually runs

the numbers behind that picture

  • 4 queries per question, written by the model
  • each query returns 6 passages plus the one either side of each — 20% of what gets sent matched nothing at all, it is just neighbours of things that matched
  • 43 passages pooled, about 13,000 words
  • of those 43, about 8 can actually answer the question and 33 cannot
  • the model draws on about 4 of the 43

The question set: 100 questions, fixed, stratified across three difficulties — answerable from one passage, from several in one video, from passages in different videos. Same 100 every run, so arms are comparable.

Where they came from: each question was generated from a passage of his transcript, paired with his own words as the reference answer. So every question is answerable, and none is out of his domain.

3  The metrics, and the one that is wrong

fixed — retrieval

Retrieval used to be scored by whether it returned the exact passages his reference answer happened to use. He covers the same ground across many videos, so a passage that genuinely answers the question was counted as a failure twice over. Now a judge reads each passage against the question instead. Precision went from 0.080 to 0.202 — the old number was understating it two and a half times.

not fixed — the answer

The answer judge still treats his one reference answer as the only correct answer. So an answer built from other things he really said, which answers the question well, is scored as wrong — either as adding something or as missing something.

The evidence that this is broken: across 600 answers, not one has ever been marked as matching. Zero, in every arm, at every setting. A metric that cannot return its own pass value is not measuring quality. It is measuring distance from one particular answer.

So the two numbers being optimised are the wrong two numbers, and that is a better explanation for eight failed attempts than anything else in this document.

What the fix is

Same shape as the retrieval fix. Ask the judge whether the answer answers the question, coherently, from his material — not whether it matches one reference.

  • missing should mean it left out something the question needed, not something he happened to say elsewhere
  • contradicts is already right — reversing something he said is wrong however you measure it

Of the two goal metrics, contradicts survives the correction and missing does not.

The pattern is consistent. Every control that gave the model less made it worse. Every control that gave it more information made it better. Narrowing failed because the model is already narrow — it reads four passages out of forty-three.

6  Controls not yet tried

  • Make it search again for what is missing. The five-round budget exists and is never used. Nothing has made it use round two.
  • Make it read every passage separately, then write from the notes. Forces all fifteen good passages through instead of four.
  • Check the answer against the passages and cut unsupported claims.
  • Drop the 20% of passages that matched nothing — pure padding from the neighbour expansion.
  • Train a small model to do the relevance judging, so the one control that clearly worked is affordable at serve time. There are 16,717 judged passage-question pairs on disk already.

Four assertions were added so the next one raises instead of scoring. The other thing missing is a noise floor: no arm has been run twice, so no one knows how much of a 2 or 3 point difference is real.

8  The state, in five lines

  1. Retrieval is fine. It was never the problem; the old metric was scoring it against the wrong thing.
  2. The model reads about four passages out of forty-three, whatever it is given, and roughly a third of its claims rest on passages that cannot answer the question.
  3. One control clearly works: telling it which passages are relevant. Contradictions fall by a third.
  4. Missing-content has not improved beyond the cutoff change, and the metric for it is broken anyway.
  5. The answer judge needs correcting before any further control is worth testing, or the search is being run against the wrong target.
