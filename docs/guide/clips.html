Stage 3 · engines/audio2face/corpus

Clean, tracked clips of their face

Everything the face stages learn comes from this: short stretches of video where we know, frame by frame, the 3D shape and position of the creator's head. Getting there from an edited YouTube video is mostly about throwing the right footage away.

What "tracked" means

The tracker is VHAP. It fits FLAME, a standard 3D model of a human head, to every frame. FLAME is a mesh whose shape is set by a few hundred numbers: some fixed for the person (how wide the jaw is), some changing every frame (mouth open, brow raised), plus where the head is and which way it points.

Why a YouTube video cannot go straight in

VHAP expects one unbroken shot from one fixed camera. An edited video has cuts, side panels, graphics and screenshots with other people's faces. None of these makes the tracker fail; each makes it return a confident wrong answer.

Commands

./alive build <creator> --stage clips runs it, stopping where a person must look. By hand, from engines/audio2face/, with the tracker's environment:

~/miniconda3/envs/vhap/bin/python corpus/status.py --takes takes/<folder>   # every video: done so far, next command
~/miniconda3/envs/vhap/bin/python corpus/status.py --seq <take>             # one video

A new creator starts as a copy of recipes/face-clips/profiles/_template.py. Its numbers (how transparent a graphic is, where a side panel sits, how short a shot can be) are read off the creator's own footage, because they differ between channels: corpus/derive_constants.py --seq <take> prints each measured distribution with a suggested cut-off, and you type the value you choose into the profile. A suggestion is a starting point: on Dr K's second video it suggested 0.325 for one value where 0.62 was right. The full recipe, with every measurement from Dr K's three videos and Huberman's one, is engines/audio2face/corpus/recipes/face-clips/RECIPE.md.

Labelling the groups

The grouping step writes one sheet per group to data/face/corpus/chunks/<take>/clusters_full/: 8 frames, with the face detector's box in red. Open them in any image viewer and write, by hand, one line per group into creators/<id>/build/clips/<take>/verdicts.json:

{
 "0":  {"verdict": "keep", "kind": "centred", "note": "clean full-frame talking head"},
 "3":  {"verdict": "drop", "kind": "graphic", "note": "title card"},
 "7":  {"verdict": "keep", "kind": "panel",   "note": "him on the right, screenshot left"},
 "11": {"verdict": "drop", "kind": "wrong_face", "note": "red box on a man in a screenshot"}
}

Every group gets a line. kind is centred or panel for kept groups.

When unsure, dropA good frame thrown away costs a fraction of a percent of hours of footage. A bad frame kept is fitted into the head shape that every other chunk then inherits. Dr K's third video, a livestream, lost 36% to this rule, mostly a webcam inset too small to use.
