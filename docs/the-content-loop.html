The Content Loop

One model that understands its own machinery, one judge that only reports facts, twelve controls, and a way to learn from a thousand questions which controls are worth reaching for.

alive / engines / knowledge_style · 17 September 2026 · design document

This describes the content half of the system: the part that takes a question and produces the substance of an answer. It stops before voice and before style. Everything here is either measured on 100 questions against a labelled archive, or explicitly marked as not yet measured.

The whole shape, before any part of it

The invariant: two intelligences, two ways in

Everything below is constrained by this. It is the strongest claim in the design and the easiest to check.

Why two and not one: the controller cannot judge itself. Asked whether it had enough, it said yes on 100 out of 100 questions. Why two and not seven: every extra role is a separate call with less context than the controller has, and each one measured worse than the controller doing the same job — the hand-written gap check found 79% of needed passages against the controller's own 85%.

The words used here

Every term below means exactly one thing in this document.

termmeans
archiveThe creator's transcripts, cut into 292 passages of about 294 words each, from 17 videos. 86,000 words total.
passageOne of those 292 pieces. Has a video id and a timestamp. Also called a verbatim, because it is his actual words.
poolThe passages retrieved so far for one question, numbered so the model can refer to them stably across turns.
word overlap searchThe matching method. Scores a passage by how many of the query's words it contains, weighting rare words more. No neural network, no embeddings. Deterministic: the same query always returns the same passages.
kHow many top-scoring passages one search returns. Currently 6. Each one also drags in its immediate neighbours from the same video, so k=6 yields roughly 12–18 passages per query.
the controllerThe model that runs the loop. Writes queries, sets the controls, reads the judge, decides when to stop, and writes the substance. One conversation from start to finish.
the judgeA separate model call with no memory of the controller's reasoning. Reports facts about the answer. Decides nothing.
a controlA parameter of the machinery that changes what happens: k, how many search rounds, which passages reach the assembler, and so on. There are twelve.
the oracleA hand-built answer key: for each question, which passages in the entire archive a real answer actually needs. Built by judging all 292 passages against every question, then re-judging the survivors strictly. Averages 3.2 passages per question. Used only for measurement, never at answer time.
tuningRunning the loop over many questions, seeing which controls actually helped, and writing that finding into the controller's instructions. Offline. Changes prompts, not weights.

The chain, and what is adjustable in it

Two models, and the wall between them

The controller cannot judge itself. This is not a principle, it is a measurement: asked "do you have enough to answer?" with 43 passages in hand, it said yes on 100 out of 100 questions. Given search tools and explicit permission to use them, it searched zero times in 100. A separate run of the same system reported its answers were 93% supported while contradicting the creator 11% of the time.

What each part of the system can possibly know

This determines what any judge is allowed to claim. A judge placed after the pool sees the pool — it has no view of the other 250 passages, so it cannot say the archive lacks something.

the consequence

"I haven't covered that" is a claim about 292 passages. No judge can license it. The system may only say it after searching and failing — and honestly, only after recording which queries were tried and that their words appear nowhere in the archive.

Today the system will say it on a judge's verdict about 41 passages. That is a claim about the pool passed off as a claim about the archive.

Where every configuration sits today

Two things trade against each other. Found is how many of the passages an answer needs were actually retrieved. Useful fraction is how much of what was retrieved matters at all. Every configuration we have buys the first by giving up the second.

Requirement one: the controller must understand its own machinery

A controller told only "you may search again" is guessing. It needs to know how each stage actually works, what it can change about it, and what changing it has been measured to do. That is a real file handed to it — harness/modules.md — and every stage gets the same five parts.

One stage, as the controller actually receives it

## Stage 2 -- SEARCH  (no model, fully deterministic)

HOW IT WORKS
   Each passage is scored by how many of your query's words it contains,
   rare words counting far more than common ones. The top k are kept.
   Then each kept passage pulls in its neighbours from the same video.
   So k=6 yields 12 to 18 passages, not 6.

   The neighbours exist because the archive is cut into 300-word pieces
   at arbitrary points. A thought he develops over two minutes lands
   across three passages; without neighbours you get the middle of an
   argument with its setup and conclusion missing.

THE MECHANISM THAT MATTERS MOST
   It is deterministic. The same query returns exactly the same passages,
   every time. So:
     - repeating a query is ALWAYS wasted
     - a query that found nothing has two different problems with two
       different fixes: wrong words (rephrase) or too tight a net (raise k)

YOUR CONTROLS
   k            default 6, settable per search
   neighbours   default plus or minus 1
   ordering     transcript order

MEASURED
   k=6 -> k=25: passages found 67% -> 89%,
                useful fraction 5.7% -> 2.3%
   At k=25 you receive 128 passages of which about 3 matter.
   The trade is question-dependent: broad questions want a wide net,
   narrow follow-ups want a tight one. Opening wide then following up
   tight has never been tested, because k has always been a constant.

UNTESTED
   neighbour width. It has been plus or minus 1 forever.

and one clarification that changes the picture

The query writer is not a separate module — it is the controller. Writing and rephrasing queries is something the controller does, not something it calls. Today they are separate model calls, which is precisely why the loop cannot reach the good query writer and improvises its own instead. That gap is worth 6 points of coverage: 79% against 85%.

Requirement two: the controller must turn findings into an ordered plan

A judge may return five findings at once. Acting on them one at a time, in the order received, wastes turns — three unsupported claims often share one missing passage, and one search fixes all three.

# turning a judge report into an action list

findings = judge(question, answer, pool, filtered)

# 1. drop the ones you disagree with, and say why. A flagged extension
#    is honest, and the faithfulness check marks it unsupported by design.
kept = [f for f in findings if not controller.rejects(f)]

# 2. group by what would fix them, not by what they are.
#    Three unsupported claims about the same thing are ONE search.
actions = group_by_fix(kept)

# 3. order by measured likelihood, not by the judge's order
actions.sort(key=prior)      # requery 63%  >  filter  >  reassemble 2%

# 4. act, cheapest first, and stop when the budget is spent
for a in actions:
    if out_of_budget(): break
    apply(a)                 # requery / adjust k / adjust filter / rewrite

# 5. if requerying has been exhausted and the words come back absent from
#    the archive, THEN the honest answer is "I haven't covered that" --
#    and the queries tried are recorded as the evidence for saying so.
why step 1 matters more than it looks

The faithfulness check counts an honest, flagged extension as unsupported, deliberately. A controller that simply complies will delete good material to satisfy it. One configuration scored the worst unsupported-claim rate of any arm — 42% — precisely because its instructions invited honest extension and the check penalised it.

So rejections are recorded. If the controller keeps rejecting the same class of finding, the judge is wrong, not the answer — which is how three broken judges would have been caught in hours rather than days.

How the controls get tuned on a thousand questions

There are two loops at completely different timescales, and conflating them is the mistake this section exists to prevent.

What tuning actually does, in order

# the tuning loop. Runs offline. Output is prompt text, not a model.

# STEP 0 -- without this, nothing below is interpretable
noise = run_twice(baseline)          # same setting, two runs
# the only noise figure we have is on a RETIRED measure: the same
# configuration twice gave 24 and 27 on one count, 14 and 12 on another.
# So a 3-point difference is weather, not weather-change.

# STEP 1 -- one control at a time, so an effect has one cause
for control in TWELVE_CONTROLS:
    for value in candidates(control):
        answers = run(questions, {**defaults, control: value})
        score   = four_measures(answers, oracle)
        if beats(score, defaults_score, by=noise):
            record(control, value, score)

# STEP 2 -- which findings should route to which control.
#            This is the real object of the search.
for q in questions:
    f = judge(q, answers[q])
    for finding in f:
        try_each_fix(finding)        # requery / k / filter / reassemble
        record(finding.kind, which_fix_worked)

priors = frequency_table(finding.kind -> fix_that_worked)
# this is where "requery first, 63% of the time" comes from

# STEP 3 -- write it down as prose the controller can read
prompt_block = render(recorded_defaults, priors, known_bad_directions)

# STEP 4 -- spend the held-out set ONCE
final = run(held_out_250, prompt_block)
# if it does not hold up here, the gains were fitted to the tuning set

This asymmetry is worth exploiting immediately: the answering machine can be tuned at full scale today, because its two measures compare the answer against the question and the passages and need no answer key. Retrieval tuning is limited by how much of the archive has been labelled.

What is measured, in plain terms

#plainlythe standard nameblamescan it run live?
1it said something no passage supportsfaithfulnessassemblyyes
2a passage that answers this existed and was never foundcontext recallretrievalno — needs the answer key
3a useful passage was found and then ignoredcontext utilizationassemblyno — needs the answer key
4it said things nobody asked aboutanswer relevancybothyes

Each is reported as the share of questions that fail it, with the raw counts beside it. No single score, no weighted total — because a change that trades a retrieval failure for an assembly failure is not an improvement, and one number would hide that.

the structural limit this imposes

Measures 2 and 3 need the answer key, so they can never be feedback at answer time. The controller can be told "you invented this" and "this wasn't asked about". It can never be told "you missed a passage that exists."

Coverage therefore cannot be corrected after the fact — it has to be got right by searching well. That is the whole reason the query writer is the most important control.

Rules that keep the measurement honest

  1. The judge sees what the model saw — no more, no less. Five separate bugs have come from breaking this: a baseline quoted from the wrong configuration, a judge truncated at 60,000 characters, a judge shown the deployed passages instead of the arm's own, a setting read from a command line instead of the run, and a filtered configuration that recorded its whole pool. Every one flattered whatever was newest.
  2. Run it twice or it is not a result. The one noise figure available is on a retired measure and is worth about 3 points. Nothing smaller counts.
  3. The answer key never enters the loop. It is the ruler. A ruler inside the thing being measured makes every number meaningless.
  4. 250 questions stay untouched until a configuration is being committed to. They overlap the tuning set by only 39 questions, so they are genuinely separate.
  5. Every arm's answers are kept on disk. Five measures have already been retired; each retirement would have meant regenerating everything if the answers had not survived.

How the code is actually structured

Everything above is the design. This section is what exists on disk, so the two can be compared. They do not fully agree, and the disagreements are named.

read this first

There are two runners, not one, and they are structured differently. run.py is the arm that currently scores best and it loads its instructions from markdown files. ladder.py is the arm that is provably equivalent to the deployed system and it holds its prompts inline.

And modules.md — the operator's manual in the section above — is loaded by no code at all. It is a design artifact written in this conversation and not yet wired to either runner.

The control flow of ladder.run()

# harness/ladder.py:331 -- the real structure, condensed but not simplified

BUDGET      = 6   # turns in the tool loop
GAP_ROUNDS  = 3   # gap-check rounds at level 4
L5_ROUNDS   = 2   # level 5 trades a round for the filter's latency

def run(item, bm, chunks, k, expand, head, level, rubric, parts):

    convo, got = ab.searches(item, ...)      # THE SHARED OWNER. 41 passages.
    body = head + ["=== QUESTION ===", item.q] + [parts_block(parts)?] + [convo]

    # ---------- LEVEL 0: the deployed call, nothing added ----------
    if level == 0:
        p   = body + [CLOSE_0, agent.FINAL]   # CLOSE_0 IS the deployed wording
        raw = llm.call(p)                     # one call, no tools
        return ask.parse(raw)["answer"]       # pull the <answer> block

    # ---------- LEVELS 1+: a session holding the same passages ----------
    s = tools.Session(item.q, k=k, expand=expand)
    for q in item.queries: s.search(q)        # same queries the deployed path used
    seeded = set(s.by_num)                    # so "new" means genuinely new

    close, gaps, shown_ids = CLOSE_1, [], None

    # ---------- LEVEL 4+: gap-check rounds, executed BEFORE the model answers ----
    if level >= 4:
        for _ in range(L5_ROUNDS if level >= 5 else GAP_ROUNDS):
            found = gaps_in(item.q, have)       # "MISSING: x | QUERY: y", max 2
            if not found: break                # it replied FULL
            before = set(s.by_num)
            for miss, query in found: s.search(query)
            if set(s.by_num) == before: break  # nothing new: searching again is waste

        # ---------- LEVEL 5: the filter. The one stage that BREAKS byte-equality ----
        if level >= 5:
            kept, dropped = filtered(s, item.q)  # relevance judge, keep YES+PART
            convo      = render(s, kept)          # re-render ONLY what survived
            body       = head + [question] + [convo]
            shown_ids  = [chunk_id of each kept]  # what the assembler ACTUALLY saw

        close = "these things were missing, they were searched for, here they are"
                # or "everything the question needs is above"

    # ---------- LEVEL 3 only: gate on the question having several parts ----------
    elif level == 3 and len(parts) > 1:
        gaps  = uncovered(parts, got)            # per part: YES / THIN / NO
        close = "these parts are not covered: ... search for them"

    # ---------- the tool loop ----------
    msgs = [{"role": "user", "content": body + [TOOLS_NOTE, close, agent.FINAL]}]
    read = set(s.by_num) if level >= 4 else seeded

    for turn in range(BUDGET):
        if BUDGET - turn <= 2:
            msgs += [user: "{n} turns left. Answer now with what you have."]

        text, calls, _ = llm.call_tools(head_system(), msgs, tool_schema())

        # THE ANSWER IS PROSE, recognised wherever it appears -- alongside tool
        # calls included. Requiring a tool call threw away 16 finished answers
        # across 10 questions, and each rewrite shed specifics.
        if "<answer>" in text:
            return ask.parse(text)["answer"], {... "chunk_ids": shown_ids or had(...)}

        if not calls:
            if len(text.split()) >= 90: return text        # unformatted but complete
            msgs += [user: "Search, read, or write the final answer."]
            continue

        for c in calls:                          # execute: search(query) / read(numbers)
            out = s.search(c.query) or render(s, c.numbers)
            results += [tool_result: out]

        # ---------- LEVEL 2+: the sufficiency judge. FORCED, not offered ----------
        if level >= 2 and read_something_new:
            verdict, detail = judge(item.q, passages, rubric)
            results += [text: "ENOUGH, write it now"
                            | "NOT COVERED: say so plainly"
                            | "NOT ENOUGH. Missing: {detail}. Search for that."]

        msgs += [user: results]

    return None, {"error": "budget exhausted"}

The harness as it will be built

One controller holding one conversation. One judge role, called at two moments. Guarantees written as code, choices written as prompt. Everything the controller learns stays in its context, including the inner loop.

The runner, in pseudocode

# harness/loop.py -- replaces run.py and ladder.py

SECONDS      = 60      # the whole budget, not a turn count
MAX_RETRIES  = 1       # at 60s there is time for about one

def answer(question, creator):
    # TWO creator-data routes, and only two
    system = read("modules.md") + read("controller.md") + persona(creator)
    s      = Session(question)          # the pool: stable numbers, dedup
    msgs   = [user: question]
    state  = {searched: 0, retries: 0, findings: []}

    while time_left(SECONDS):
        text, calls = llm.call_tools(system, msgs, TOOLS,
                                     cache_prefix=True)   # the stable prefix
        msgs += [assistant: text, calls]
        out = []

        for c in calls:

            # ---- the inner loop: retrieval measuring itself ----
            if c.name == "search":
                mark = set(s.by_num)
                r    = s.search(c.query, k=c.k or 6)     # k IS the model's choice
                new  = set(s.by_num) - mark
                state.searched += 1

                # NO JUDGE HERE. The controller has the passages, the new-count
                # and the absent-words line in front of it; deciding "proceed or
                # rewrite" needs no verdict. All of it is appended so it
                # ACCUMULATES -- otherwise the absent-words line is emitted and
                # forgotten, and it can re-issue a query it already tried against
                # a deterministic index.
                out += [r.index,                          # n new / n already held
                        r.absent_words,                   # free: index lookup
                        s.first_lines(new)]               # free: already in the pool

            elif c.name == "read":
                out += [s.read(c.numbers)]      # NO 12,000-char cap. It silently
                                                # truncated 84% of reads: asked 26,
                                                # received 7.

            # No filter stage and no filter judge. Choosing which passages to
            # use is part of assembling, and the controller already has them.
            # A filter judge was 7 calls and ~20s of a 60s budget: level 1 ran
            # 17s, level 5 with it ran 38-48s. Whether the controller chooses as
            # well is an EXACT offline count -- precision against the answer key
            # -- so it does not need a judge in the loop to find out.

            # ---- the outer loop: one judgement, then the retry branch ----
            elif c.name == "answer":
                if state.searched == 0:          # GUARANTEE, in code.
                    out += ["Search first."]      # Left free, it skips retrieval and
                    continue                     # answers from its own knowledge.

                findings = judge_answer(question, c.text,
                                        s.pool, s.labels)   # no history
                if not findings or state.retries >= MAX_RETRIES:
                    return c.text                # accepted, or out of retries
                state.retries += 1

                # findings append to the SAME conversation. The controller
                # already knows what it searched and wrote, so planning costs
                # no extra call -- which is the whole argument for one brain.
                state.findings += findings
                out += [render(findings)]

        msgs += [user: out]

    return best_so_far(msgs)      # budget spent: return the last draft

Guarantees are code. Choices are prompt.

This is what answers "what if it is too free". The failure mode measured is not chaos — it is inertia: given tools and permission, it searched 0.00 times in 100 questions. So the minimum actions are if statements the model cannot decline, and everything else is open.

The sanity check, before any quality claim

Whether the answers are good is a separate question from whether the machinery is being used. This section is the second one, and it has to pass first — a quality number from a controller that is improvising means nothing.

Run on 5 questions drawn from the 100 every other arm was measured on, with the full conversation and the chain of thought recorded per question. Five questions is a read, not a measurement; every number below should be treated that way.

Does it know that searching is word-matching, so it must write in his idiom?

Yes, and the chain of thought shows it deciding this before acting, in 5 of 5 questions. Its own words:

"I should search using his own vocabulary -- terms like feeling stuck,
 restarting your life, whether it's too late, puer aeternus, taking the
 plunge, waiting and stagnation... framing the queries in his idiom."

"I should search using his specific terminology -- discipline as a verb,
 willpower, controlled versus autonomous motivation, action crisis."

"I'll phrase the underlying question in his own terms."

And the behaviour matches the intent: 93% of its query words exist in the archive, only 8% are lifted from the asker's question.

One question went the other way, deliberately — on catcalling it reasoned "phrasing my queries in the viewer's own vocabulary". That looks like a violation and is probably correct: the archive literally contains "catcalls, whistles and stares", so there the viewer's words were his words. A subtler rule than the prompt states, which the controller appears to have worked out.

Did it act on the judge, or just retry?

It planned. Three examples from its reasoning on receiving findings:

what it didits own words
triaged by cost"That's a simple word swap I can accept — 'most dudes' to 'a lot of dudes' — no need to re-query."
flagged instead of deleting"marking the 'don't schedule a reflection day' idea as my own extension rather than something grounded"

That third one is the rule this document sets out being followed exactly: "I haven't covered that" is a claim about 292 passages, and may only be said after searching and getting nothing.

Two behavioural faults

It batches the first turn and then stops batching. The turn shapes, where 4s0r means four searches and no reads:

therapy            4s0r 0s1r 2s0r 0s1r ANS 1s1r ANS 2s0r 0s1r ANS   10 turns  219s
mental health day  4s0r 2s1r 2s1r 1s1r 1s1r 0s1r 1s0r ANS ...       14 turns  337s
discipline         4s0r 0s1r 0s1r ANS                                4 turns   72s

A third of all turns make exactly one call, and each turn is a ~22 second round trip. Batching the same work into two turns instead of six would have saved roughly 86 seconds on one question. Turns averaged 8.2 against a designed 4, and time 177s against a 60s budget.

And on one question it retried twice without searching at all. The pool stayed at 54 passages across all three judgements — it rewrote rather than re-queried, against its own stated prior that retrieval causes 63% of failures and the assembler ignoring material causes 2%.

Auditing the judge

The judge was checked the same way — against the passages it was shown, not against its own confidence. It is reliable on one fault type and not the other.

fault typewhat it asksreliability
UNSUPPORTEDdoes a passage state this claimstrong
OFFTOPICwas this asked aboutnot exercised enough to say
MISSINGwas something asked and not answeredweak — 1 of 4 flatly wrong, 1 oscillated

Where it was right

It caught the answer asserting "I don't use the word placebo; that's the frame I'd use instead" — a first-person claim about the speaker's own vocabulary with no passage behind it. Its reasoning traced every other claim in that answer to a numbered passage and then isolated this one:

"The closing line, 'I don't use the word placebo,' is a claim about the
 speaker's own vocabulary and practice. No passage in the list mentions
 placebo at all, and none says anything about the speaker avoiding or
 rejecting that term. It is asserted in the speaker's voice as a statement
 about himself with no basis in the supplied material."

It also caught "most men don't think catcalling is a big deal" as an overstatement of what the passage supports; the controller accepted "a lot of dudes". That is the granularity this document asks for — a named claim and a named passage, not a score.

Where it was wrong

Flatly wrong once. It reported that the catcalling answer "never states what catcalling actually does to the person experiencing it". The answer says:

"Imagine someone you're not attracted to. Now imagine that person, every
 few days to once a month, stares at you, makes sexual advances, touches
 you in ways you don't want. Once? Fine, you can handle it once.
 What does every few days do to your psychology?"

It wanted the effect stated and got it evoked — by the same thought-experiment device the creator uses in the passage itself.

And it oscillated. On the mental-health-day question it raised the same MISSING in judgement 1, cleared it in judgement 2, then raised it again in judgement 3 — on an answer that by then contained a direct prescription ("point the day at actions. Two questions worth the whole day…"). Same fault, three reads, two verdicts.

this is the fourth completeness judge to fail here

Span overlap against the source passage. The holistic "does it answer the question" judge. The decomposer that agreed with an independent one on only 57 of 100 questions. Now MISSING.

Grounding judges keep working; completeness judges keep breaking. Four attempts is a pattern, not three unlucky prompts. The difference looks structural: UNSUPPORTED has to name a claim and a passage, so it cannot report an impression. MISSING has no anchor, so it does.

What is not known

open questionwhy it matters
Is the loop better than simply turning the existing rounds up?Every "deployed" baseline ran 1 round against a default of 5. So "the loop wins" may partly mean "a loop beats no loop", which was never in doubt. This is the first thing to settle.
What is the noise on the four measures?Without it, no tuning result is interpretable. Prerequisite, not part of the search.
Does the filter help the finished answer?Its mechanism is measured — 62% smaller, nothing needed lost. Its effect on the answer is not. One run recorded the wrong passage set and had to be thrown away.
Does retrying after a judgement help at all?No configuration has ever done it. It is the centre of this design and has zero measurements behind it.
Is word-overlap search the real ceiling?The controller already writes nine good queries in his own vocabulary and still finds only 86% of what is needed. That points at the matching method, not the querying.
Can the style loop be closed?Not yet. Its judge scores 98–100% on everything tried, so it cannot rank two outputs and cannot drive a loop. Style is deliberately out of scope until that is fixed.
